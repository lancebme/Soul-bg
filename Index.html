The Myth of the Maverick

Contemporary Periodical Series

Survival strategies are rarely solo acts. My survival strategies are highly effective yet profoundly inefficient, relying entirely on communal support and alms rather than individual autonomy. It was never the plan; yet, somehow, it tragically became the reality. Unlike you, who followed a more traditional path into an excellent, stable, and sustainable survival strategy based on forming mutually beneficial collaborations with legacy institutions, many of us operate in a different framework.

The Biological Truth: Interdependence

Consider the start of it all: no individual breastfeeds or cleans themselves at birth. Our social architecture is fundamentally multinodal and networked—humanity is an interconnected, highly social, team-oriented, and communal species. In this framework, total isolation from the pack does not mean death; it means the complete ablation of one's genetic distinctness from the evolutionary record.

"True agency is found in how we navigate our connections, not how we sever them."

Challenging the Myth

Radical Individualism is at best a conceptual misnomer and at worst a destructive delusion. By attempting to eliminate the inevitable friction of interpersonal life, this ideology relies on an eschatological grandiosity suited for fiction rather than serious critique. While interpersonal tension is constant and palpable, it cannot justify dissolving the social contract that constitutes the warp and weft of human civilization.

The Necessary Synthesis

Ultimately, the goal is not to abandon the self, but to locate the self within the collective. If we accept that our survival is fundamentally tied to the health of the group, then the "friction" of interpersonal life—the disagreements, the compromises, the shared burdens—ceases to be a nuisance to be avoided. Instead, it becomes the vital energy that drives human progress.

We must move past the exhausted fantasy of the self-made man or the isolated sovereign. A more mature, sustainable path involves recognizing that true agency is found in how we navigate our connections, not how we sever them. By leaning into the discomfort of community, we do not surrender our individuality; we secure it. We are, and have always been, a species that thrives only when we acknowledge that the strongest "individual" is the one who knows exactly how much they rely on everyone else.
